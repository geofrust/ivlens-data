IV Lens Privacy Policy

Effective 21 November 2026

IV Lens doesn't collect any personal data. There are no accounts, ads, analytics or tracking. Your Pokémon collection and settings stay on your phone, and nothing about you is sent to us or anyone else. This covers IV Lens for iPhone and for Android; what differs on Android is below.

What the app reads on your iPhone

  • Your screen, while you record it. When you start a screen broadcast to IV Lens, the app reads Pokémon GO's appraisal screen to work out each Pokémon's stats. Text recognition uses Apple's on-device Vision framework. Frames are analysed in memory and thrown away; they are never uploaded.
  • Saved frames, only if you turn it on. With "Save missed frames" on, frames the scanner couldn't read are kept in the app's storage on your iPhone so you can look at them. They stay there until you delete them, and leave your iPhone only if you share them yourself.
  • A picture of each Pokémon you scan. The app keeps a small picture of the Pokémon, cropped from the appraisal screen, to show in your collection. It stays on your iPhone and is deleted with the Pokémon (the next time the app opens, so an undo can still bring it back), or all at once in Settings › Display.
  • Screenshots and recordings you pick. Images and videos you choose with the photo picker or the Files app are read on your iPhone. The app sees only the items you pick, not the rest of your library.

What it stores

  • Your collection, notes, settings and a daily backup, all on your iPhone. The backups are in the Files app under On My iPhone › IV Lens › Backups.
  • If iCloud Backup is on, your iPhone's backup includes this data. That's Apple's service, covered by Apple's privacy policy.
  • Deleting the app deletes all of it, apart from any copies you exported yourself.

What it downloads

About once a week, and when you tap Update in Settings, the app downloads game data and battle rankings from geofrust.github.io/ivlens-data, which is hosted on GitHub Pages. These requests contain no personal information. Like any website, GitHub receives your IP address and basic request details; see the GitHub General Privacy Statement.

When you open the Dashboard or the Raids tab, and at most every 6 hours, the app also downloads the current raid bosses and the raid and Max Battle event calendar and Giovanni's current team compiled by LeekDuck, from the ScrapedDuck project on GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com), and Snacknap's page of current Max Battles (snacknap.com). These requests also contain no personal information. The app makes no other internet connections.

IV Lens for Android

The Android app works like the iPhone app, with these differences:

  • Reading your screen. When you tap Start scanning, Android asks whether to share your screen with IV Lens; while it's shared, a notification says IV Lens is scanning, and you can stop it there at any time. The app reads Pokémon GO's appraisal screen with Google's ML Kit text recognition, which runs on the phone. Frames are analysed in memory and thrown away; they are never uploaded.
  • The floating card. With your permission to display over other apps, IV Lens shows each scan in a small card over the game. It draws only IV Lens's own card and reads nothing through this permission.
  • Pictures. To find the Pokémon in a screen for its picture, the app uses ML Kit's subject segmentation, which Google Play services downloads to your phone; the picture is made on the phone.
  • ML Kit diagnostics. Google's ML Kit libraries send Google basic device and app information and performance metrics (for example the phone model, the app's version and how long a step took), for diagnostics and usage analytics. They don't send your screens, your Pokémon or anything that identifies you. See Google's ML Kit data disclosure.
  • Storage and backups. Your collection, pictures and settings stay in the app's storage on your phone. A daily backup is saved in Downloads › IV Lens Backups, where it stays if you remove the app. Android's own backup to Google Drive is turned off for IV Lens.
  • Notifications. Scan banners and event reminders (an hour before Community Days, raid hours and other events, which you can turn off in Settings) are created on your phone; they don't use any push service.
  • Downloads. The same as on iPhone: game data and rankings from the IV Lens data mirror, and raid bosses, events, Giovanni's team and the current Max Battles as described above.

What you share

Data leaves the app only when you choose to send it: for example sharing a CSV export, a backup or a Pokémon summary, or copying a search string. It goes wherever you send it.

Notifications

Scan banners are local notifications created on your phone. They don't use any push service.

Children

IV Lens collects no personal data from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
